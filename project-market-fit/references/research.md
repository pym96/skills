# Research a decision

## 1. Name the uncertainty

State which unknown changes the fit judgment or investment choice, such as whether the role owns execution reliability or model evaluation.

**Done when:** The research question names a decision, not merely a topic to survey.

## 2. Inspect demand evidence

Start with supplied JDs and feedback, then investigate current first-party sources: careers pages, product documentation, engineering posts, repositories, papers, or talks. Use reposts and industry commentary as leads. A keyword does not establish a bottleneck, and published research does not establish a current hiring priority.

Update the existing fit map rather than creating a second demand report. In each relevant entry, record the supporting observation, its provenance and date where available, inferred constraints, and the finding's effect on the decision. Do not attribute proposed success criteria to the employer.

For a role-family claim, seek independent examples across teams or vacancies; reposts of one JD are not independent. If sources conflict, state how each interpretation changes the recommendation. When access is limited, retain a hypothesis rather than claiming verification.

**Done when:** Decision-driving claims have traceable support or explicit uncertainty, and conflicting evidence has been addressed.

## 3. Stop and route

Stop when remaining uncertainty would not change the next action, accessible searches repeat the same evidence, or the user's research budget is reached. On inconclusive findings, identify the smallest useful next validation rather than expanding the search indefinitely.

**Done when:** The map identifies what the research changed and which gap action follows. A bibliography alone is not a result.
